# Railyard — Competitive Positioning, User Research, Technical Architecture Notes

Date: 2026-10-23. Companion to `docs/RAILYARD_ENTERPRISE_ARCHITECTURE.md` (full
architecture), `docs/CONSUMERS.md` (who calls what today) and
`docs/OSS_FOUNDATIONS.md` (build-vs-fork decisions). `PLAN-2026-10-23-DEEP.md`
did not exist in this repo at time of writing, so there is nothing to avoid
duplicating; this doc stays on competitive positioning, user research and
architecture as instructed, not a pages/APIs/features backlog.

## What Railyard actually is today (read from the live code, not the pitch)

- Railyard is infrastructure first, product second: per `docs/CONSUMERS.md`,
  "Railyard exists first to run billing, LLM routing, metering and payouts
  for OUR portfolio (Rally, Menivor, Scribase, OpenJev, the mobile apps and
  games), and second to sell the same services to others on the same code
  path." Every hop is opt-in/env-gated; most of it is wired but not switched
  on in production for the owner's own apps yet.
- The only component with a live, no-login, self-serve signup funnel today
  is **Insights** (`apps/insights-edge`): public landing page, email+domain
  form, 10,000 free events/month, instant tokenized dashboard link, running
  at `insights.ereboslab.com` (verified live, HTTP 200, 2026-10-23).
- **Faultline** (GlitchTip, `errors.ereboslab.com`) and **Rithub** (Gitea
  fork, `git.ereboslab.com`) are both live (verified HTTP 200) but have no
  external self-serve signup wired — Faultline is consumed by the owner's
  other products directly; Rithub runs native Gitea accounts with no OIDC/SSO
  bridge to the rest of Railyard yet (`apps/gitea/README.md`: "No
  Railyard-run OIDC/identity server exists yet").
- **apps/console** is the one place a unified "Railyard" product exists: sign
  in with Google/GitHub/email, a $1 refundable card check, a 14-day trial,
  one monthly/yearly plan via Dodo, and a nav bundling **Insights, Flags,
  Product analytics, Usage, Billing**, plus **Watch** (uptime) behind a flag
  and an outbound **Code** link to Rithub. This is the closest thing to a
  PostHog/Vercel-style bundle Railyard has — and per `apps/console/NOTES.md`
  it is built and merged to `main` but not deployed to the live Worker,
  blocked on one owner-only step (provisioning a real `railyard-console`
  Scribase project; a guard in `lib/config.ts` 500s every route until that
  happens).
- **Relay** (model gateway), **Rago** (usage billing), **Switchyard**
  (tool-calling), **Vend** (resellable AI SDK), **Mint** (ledger) have no
  self-serve signup at all — onboarding a new org today is an admin-token
  `curl` call (`apps/toolgate/README.md`'s own bootstrap example), not a
  web form.

That shape — one real self-serve wedge (Insights), five to seven backend
rails with zero self-serve path, and one unified console built but not yet
reachable by an outside user — is the ground truth this strategy is written
against.

## 1. Direct competitors

Grouped by rail, since Railyard is honestly a bundle of narrow categories,
not one product with one competitor set.

**Model gateway (Relay vs.)**
- OpenRouter — no monthly fee, 400+ models/70+ providers, no per-token
  markup; makes money on a 5.5% fee on card credit top-ups (min $0.80, 5%
  crypto), 8% for the EU/US-only Business tier. Strongest: largest catalog,
  zero-setup BYOK. Weakest: no self-hosting at all, so no data-residency
  option, and the 5.5% top-up fee compounds hard at volume (~$5k/yr on
  $100k/month of spend).
- Portkey (now marketed as Prisma AIRS AI Gateway after the May 2026 Palo
  Alto Networks acquisition) — Developer free (10K logs/mo, 3-day
  retention), Production $49/mo (100K logs/mo, 30-day retention, guardrails,
  RBAC, semantic caching, +$9/100K logs). Strongest: 1,600+
  providers/models, SOC2/HIPAA/GDPR compliance, self-hostable. Weakest:
  now enterprise-security-branded post-acquisition, $49/mo floor for
  anything past the free dev tier, and billed on log volume rather than
  being free at the gateway layer.
- Helicone — open-source, free to self-host; managed Hobby free (10K
  req/mo), Pro $79/mo, Team $799/mo. Strongest: 0% token markup, fully
  self-hostable, strong observability. Weakest: smaller catalog (~100
  models vs. OpenRouter's 400+ and Portkey's 1,600+).

**Usage/subscription billing (Rago vs.)**
- Stripe Billing — 0.7% of billed volume (0.5% at Scale), stacked on top of
  2.9%+30c card processing. Strongest: already the payments rail most
  founders use. Weakest: cost scales with revenue forever (≈$70k/yr at
  $10M in usage charges), 100 req/s Events API cap, weak hybrid pricing.
- Lago (Cloud) — Starter free up to $250K cumulative invoiced revenue, then
  0.75%; Performance $599/mo includes $100K/mo free billing volume; AGPLv3
  open-source core. Strongest: the only fully open-source option, so
  self-hostable with no per-transaction fee. Weakest: cloud version is
  thin — most real functionality needs the $599+/mo tier, and AGPL scares
  off teams that need to embed it in a closed product.
- Orb / Metronome — both custom/sales-quoted, no public pricing (Orb
  reportedly ≈$720/mo platform fee; Metronome 0.8% of billing volume +
  $0.04/1,000 events, now Stripe-owned after its 2026 $1B acquisition and
  powering OpenAI/Anthropic/NVIDIA billing). Strongest: proven at serious
  AI-usage scale. Weakest: opaque pricing, sales-cycle onboarding — exactly
  what a solo founder or 3-person team cannot get through quickly.

**Error tracking (Faultline vs.)**
- Sentry — Team $26/mo (50K errors), Business $80/mo, Enterprise custom
  (median $23,301/yr per Vendr data); 2026 added a $40/contributor/mo "Seer"
  AI add-on. Strongest: mature product, deep performance/replay/profiling.
  Weakest: per-user and per-event overage pricing gets expensive fast
  (teams of 50-200 report $500-$2,000+/mo), and self-hosting Sentry needs
  50+ Compose services and 16GB+ RAM.
- GlitchTip Cloud (the project Faultline is itself built on) — free to
  self-host unlimited; hosted free tier 1,000 events/mo, paid from
  $17-50/mo for 50K-500K events. Strongest: no per-seat fees, trivially
  light to self-host (512MB RAM). Weakest: Faultline today is just a
  deployed GlitchTip instance with no visible differentiation from
  glitchtip.com's own cloud offering — same product, same price class,
  different logo.
- Honeybadger — positioned at indie/small-team error tracking, similar
  price band to Sentry Team. Strongest: uptime+error+cron monitoring in one
  small-team plan. Weakest: smaller ecosystem, no open self-host option.

**Web analytics (Insights vs.)**
- Plausible — $9/mo for 10K pageviews/site, cookieless, EU-hosted, open
  source with a free self-hosted Community Edition, auto-detects AI
  referral traffic (ChatGPT/Perplexity/Claude). Strongest: cheapest
  single-site entry price, self-hostable. Weakest: Stats API gated to the
  Business tier; per-site pricing penalizes multi-property teams.
- Fathom — $15/mo flat for unlimited sites up to 100K views, $45/mo to 1M.
  Strongest: one flat bill across unlimited sites, forever retention and
  API access even on the entry plan. Weakest: no AI-referral attribution,
  no self-hosting (only a "Lite" self-host tier).
- PostHog — free tier covers 1M analytics events, 5K session replays, 1M
  feature-flag requests, 100K error-tracking exceptions, 1.5K survey
  responses/month, all in one account; paid tiers from $250/mo (Boost).
  Strongest: the only competitor that already bundles analytics + flags +
  replay + error tracking + surveys the way Railyard's console is trying
  to. Weakest: 2026 cut session-replay free allowance 66% (15K→5K/mo), and
  real bills jump fast past free tier (modeled $370-$1,900+/mo for a
  growing app) — the free tier is generous but the cliff is steep.
- Simple Analytics — $20/mo + $20/extra user, no self-hosting. Weakest of
  the three on both price and flexibility.

**Feature flags (Flags vs.)**
- LaunchDarkly — free Developer tier (unlimited seats/flags, 14-day
  retention, 1,000 client MAU), then pay-as-you-go from $10-12 per service
  connection/1K MAU; real bills $75-$825/mo at 10K-100K MAU, enterprise
  contracts averaging $64K-$72K/yr. Strongest: enterprise governance,
  audit, approvals. Weakest: cost explodes with MAU — the worst fit for a
  cash-constrained small team.
- Unleash — self-hosted free forever (core features, capped at 1
  project/2 environments); Cloud Pay-As-You-Go $75/seat/mo, 5-seat minimum.
  Strongest: true free self-host. Weakest: cloud entry price ($375/mo
  minimum for 5 seats) is high for a small team; no built-in analytics.
- Flagsmith — BSD-3 self-hosted with no artificial caps; hosted free tier
  1 member/50K requests/mo; Start-Up $40-45/mo for 3 members/1M requests.
  Strongest: most generous self-host (uncapped) and cheapest at scale
  among hosted options. Weakest: smaller ecosystem/integration catalog
  than LaunchDarkly.
- PostHog — flags bundled into the same free/paid tiers above (1M flag
  requests free, then $0.0001/request). Strongest: one bill with analytics.
  Weakest: same steep post-free-tier cliff.

**Uptime monitoring (Watch vs.)**
- UptimeRobot — free tier 50 monitors/5-min checks (most generous free
  tier in the category); Solo $7/mo, Team $29/mo, Enterprise $54/mo.
  Strongest: cheapest headline price, best free tier. Weakest: no built-in
  on-call/incident management — needs a bolted-on PagerDuty integration
  that adds real cost (~$2,124/yr) at team scale.
- Better Stack — free tier only 10 monitors/3-min checks; paid from
  $29/mo but bundles uptime + on-call + incident management + status
  pages in one product, which wins on 3-year TCO (~$1,800 vs. ~$2,472+ for
  UptimeRobot+PagerDuty) once a team needs on-call. Strongest: all-in-one
  incident workflow. Weakest: smaller free tier than UptimeRobot.
- Pingdom — no free tier, from $15-18/mo for 10 checks, climbs fast;
  mainly relevant if already in the SolarWinds ecosystem. Weakest in the
  category on both price and free-tier access.

**Tool-calling / agent actions (Switchyard vs.)**
- Zapier (incl. Zapier MCP) — task-based billing; Professional ≈$20-30/mo
  for 750 tasks, Team $69-103.50/mo for 2,000 tasks; 9,000+ app
  integrations; each MCP tool call burns 2 tasks. Strongest: by far the
  largest integration catalog and a no-code builder non-engineers can use.
  Weakest: task pricing is expensive per-unit for high-volume agent
  workloads, and MCP calls cost double in task consumption.
- Composio — tool-call-based billing; free 20K-100K calls/mo (depends on
  OAuth setup), Pro $29/mo usage credit then $0.0003/call + $0.003/trigger
  + 5% markup on premium tools + $3.75/M sandbox LLM tokens. Strongest:
  far better unit economics at high agent-call volume (200K calls for
  $29/mo vs. Zapier's 750 tasks for ~$20-30). Weakest: ~1,000-1,500
  toolkits vs. Zapier's 9,000+, and four separate metered dimensions make
  the bill hard to forecast.

**Git hosting (Rithub vs.)**
- GitHub Team/Enterprise — $4/user/mo Team, $21/user/mo Enterprise (a
  50-person team: ≈$12,600/yr before Copilot). Strongest: the default
  network effect — every hire and every OSS contributor already has an
  account. Weakest: no real self-hosted free path (Enterprise contract
  required); expensive per-seat at scale.
- Gitea Cloud/Enterprise — hosted from ≈$3.25-9.50/user/mo; self-hosted
  MIT core is free and runs on a $5 VPS. Strongest: MIT license, tiny
  footprint, Actions/Packages/Wiki/LFS all built in (proven locally in
  `apps/gitea`'s own 2026-10-15 session: HTTPS/SSH push, LFS, npm
  registry publish, wiki, Actions tab all confirmed working). Weakest: no
  official large-scale managed cloud comparable to GitHub's; Rithub today
  is undifferentiated Gitea branding with no SSO into the rest of
  Railyard yet.
- Codeberg — free for FOSS projects, non-profit, Forgejo-based. Not a
  commercial-team competitor; relevant only as the free alternative for
  open-source maintainers.

**All-in-one bundle (Railyard Console vs.)**
- PostHog is the one competitor that already does what `apps/console` is
  trying to do — analytics + flags + replay + error tracking + surveys,
  one sign-in, one bill, generous free tier, $250/mo first paid step.
  This is the real strategic threat to the console, not any single-purpose
  tool above.

## 2. Differentiation plan — specific, not generic

- **Settlement layer, not vanity bundling.** Every sub-rail (Relay, Rago,
  Switchyard, Vend, Turnstile) holds a Mint hold before work happens and
  captures the exact cost after, with Ed25519 hash-linked receipts and
  reserve conservation (`docs/RAILYARD_ENTERPRISE_ARCHITECTURE.md`). That
  means Railyard can make a claim none of the above can: **a failed LLM
  call, a failed tool call, or a failed task literally cannot generate a
  charge** — there is no refund queue because there is no premature debit.
  OpenRouter, Zapier, Composio and Stripe Billing all bill on attempted
  usage/tasks, not verified success; this is a structural, provable
  difference to put on a comparison page, not a tagline.
- **One bill across categories PostHog doesn't cover.** PostHog bundles
  analytics+flags+replay+error-tracking+surveys. It does **not** do LLM
  model routing, usage-based billing/invoicing, uptime monitoring, or git
  hosting. Railyard's console (once deployed) is positioned to be the
  wider bundle: the same analytics/flags/replay PostHog offers, plus
  model-gateway spend, tool-call spend, usage billing and uptime, on the
  same Mint ledger and the same $1-trial-then-one-plan billing ladder —
  i.e., compete with PostHog on scope, not on matching its free-tier
  numbers (which are genuinely hard to beat: 1M events/5K replays/1M flag
  requests free is a high bar).
- **Undercut OpenRouter's take-rate explicitly.** Relay charges no
  percentage markup on top-ups the way OpenRouter's 5.5% (8% EU/US-only)
  does. A direct "$100k/month spend → OpenRouter takes ~$5,500/yr-$8,000/yr
  in fees, Relay takes $0 markup (Mint's cut is on captured compute, not a
  credit-purchase tax)" comparison page is the concrete artifact; this is
  the single clearest price-axis win available anywhere in the portfolio.
- **Beat Gitea Cloud and GitHub Team on bundled billing, not on git
  features.** Rithub cannot out-feature GitHub. It can be the only git
  forge where CI minutes, LFS storage and package registry usage settle
  through the same Mint ledger as a team's model/tool spend — one invoice
  instead of a GitHub bill plus an OpenRouter bill plus a Stripe bill.
  That requires finishing the one real gap found in the code: Rithub has
  no SSO into the console yet (`apps/gitea/README.md`, confirmed
  TODO(railyard-sso)) — ship that before claiming "one bill, one
  sign-in" anywhere in marketing copy, or the claim is false on day one.
- **Faultline needs a reason to exist beyond "GlitchTip with a new logo."**
  Today it has none — same price class, same features as glitchtip.com's
  own cloud. The only defensible move is to fold Faultline's errors into
  the same per-org usage meter as Insights/Flags/Watch in the console
  (it is architecturally positioned to, since Rago already meters
  `insights_events` the same way) so error events are free up to the
  console's bundle cap instead of a separate GlitchTip bill — undercutting
  GlitchTip Cloud's own $17-50/mo tiers by rolling errors into a plan the
  customer is already paying for one other thing from.
- **Switchyard's success-only billing is the Composio/Zapier differentiator,
  not catalog size.** Railyard cannot out-catalog Zapier (9,000+
  integrations) or Composio (~1,000-1,500 toolkits) soon — Switchyard
  ships 27 tools today (`apps/toolgate/README.md`). The honest wedge is
  teams that already pay for Relay (model routing) wanting their tool
  calls on the *same* `sk-t0-` key and the *same* Mint ledger as their
  model spend, with Zapier's failure mode (billed task even when the
  Zap errors) replaced by hold/capture. Market it as the tool rail for
  people already on Relay, not as a general Zapier replacement.

## 3. Primary user persona / ICP

- **Who exactly:** a technical founder or 2-5 person engineering team
  shipping an AI-powered product (SaaS, agent, or app) who is already
  paying for, or about to sign up for, at least two of: an LLM gateway
  (OpenRouter/Portkey), a billing/metering tool (Stripe Billing/Lago), an
  error tracker (Sentry/GlitchTip), and a web analytics tool
  (Plausible/PostHog). This matches the owner's standing audience rule —
  founders, small teams, developers, Supabase/Neon/Resend users — and
  matches who the live products (Scribase, Menivor, Rally/vcpeer, Bidstage)
  already are inside this same portfolio.
- **Job to be done:** stop paying 3-5 separate subscriptions and
  reconciling 3-5 separate dashboards for "is my AI product working and
  am I losing money on it" — one sign-in, one usage meter, one invoice,
  across model spend, tool spend, errors, analytics and uptime.
- **What they are NOT:** not an enterprise buyer (no procurement cycle
  tolerance — LaunchDarkly/Orb/Metronome's custom-quote sales motion is
  exactly what this persona avoids), and not a no-code marketer (Zapier's
  audience) — they write code and want an API/CLI first, a dashboard
  second.
- **Where they already are today, inside this portfolio:** Scribase
  (Supabase/Neon alternative), Bidstage, Menivor and Rally/vcpeer all
  target this same founder/small-team persona — Railyard's fastest,
  cheapest acquisition channel is cross-sell into users who already signed
  up for one of those, not cold outbound to strangers.

## 4. User journey map (discovery to paying/retained)

1. **Discovery.** Today realistically happens through: (a) cross-sell from
   another owned product (Scribase/Menivor/Bidstage users seeing an
   "Insights" or "Faultline" badge), (b) a founder searching
   "OpenRouter alternative no markup" / "Sentry alternative self-hosted" /
   "Plausible vs PostHog" — the exact query shapes the research above
   turned up third-party roundups for — or (c) a comparison/listing page
   Railyard itself publishes.
2. **Landing.** Lands on one single-purpose surface — right now only
   Insights' public landing page (`insights.ereboslab.com`) actually has
   this; Faultline and Rithub have no equivalent public landing/signup
   page yet.
3. **Self-serve activation.** Email+domain form, no card, dashboard link
   mailed instantly (Insights' existing flow). This step works today and
   is the only step in the whole journey that is proven end-to-end.
4. **First real value moment.** Seeing real traffic/error/flag data in the
   dashboard within the free tier (10,000 events/mo for Insights).
5. **Second-product need.** User hits a second pain (an error spike, a
   feature-flag need, a model-spend question) and should be offered the
   console bundle instead of signing up for a competitor for that second
   need.
6. **Unified signup.** Console sign-in (Google/GitHub/email), $1 refundable
   card check, 14-day trial, see Apps/Insights/Flags/Product
   analytics/Usage/Billing in one place.
7. **Conversion.** Trial ends, one monthly/yearly plan via Dodo, tracked
   through `lib/ladder.ts` and Standard Webhooks.
8. **Retention/expansion.** Usage grows across more meters (model spend via
   Relay, tool spend via Switchyard), all settling through the same Mint
   ledger and the same invoice.

**Biggest drop-off risk, named specifically:** step 6, the unified console
signup, **cannot happen at all right now** — `apps/console` is merged to
`main` but not deployed to its live Worker, blocked on one owner step
(provisioning the `railyard-console` Scribase project so `SCRIBASE_URL`
stops pointing at the control-plane host; confirmed by a live deploy
attempt in `apps/console/NOTES.md` that 500s every route including `/`
until that happens). Every user who completes steps 1-5 on Insights today
has nowhere to go for step 6 — there is no visible path from "I like
Insights" to "I am now a Railyard console customer." Second-worst drop-off:
steps 2-3 don't exist yet for Faultline or Rithub at all, so cross-sell
from Insights into those products means handing the user an admin-API
curl command, not a signup form.

## 5. Three concrete growth moves

1. **Ship a "you're already using Insights, here's your Railyard account"
   cross-sell banner the moment the console is reachable**, pre-filling
   the email from the Insights dashboard-link flow so an existing
   self-serve Insights user becomes a console sign-in with zero new typing
   — this directly plugs the step-6 drop-off found above and costs no
   infrastructure beyond a banner + prefilled OAuth/email-code start,
   since both already share a Scribase-based auth pattern.
2. **Publish one real, numbers-backed comparison page: "Relay vs.
   OpenRouter: $0 markup vs. 5.5-8% on every top-up."** Submit it to the
   same places the research above surfaced organically ranking for this
   (awesome-llm-gateway GitHub lists, r/LocalLLaMA and r/artificial threads
   comparing gateways, Hacker News "Show HN" for a model router) — this is
   the single clearest, most defensible price-axis claim in the whole
   portfolio and nobody else in this niche is making it with Mint's
   success-only billing as the mechanism (not just a lower number, a
   structurally different guarantee).
3. **Give Faultline a reason to exist by wiring it into the console's
   usage meter before marketing it at all**, then target the exact search
   intent third-party sites are already writing about — "Sentry
   alternative for indie teams," "GlitchTip hosted" — with a page that
   says "free up to your Insights/Flags plan's cap, not a separate
   $17-50/mo bill," which is a real, buildable differentiation (Rago
   already metering `insights_events` the same way) rather than
   "we hosted the same open-source project someone else already hosts."
